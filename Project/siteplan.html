🌿 eWiseHome reduce e-waste · smart choices

domain: ewisehome.org
📛 Why "eWiseHome"? The name blends "e-waste" with "wise decisions" and "home" — emphasizing practical, household-level action. It's easy to remember, positive, and signals empowerment. The .org domain fits our educational, community-driven mission to help families tackle the e-waste crisis through repair, donation, and mindful consumption.

📌 Site Purpose

eWiseHome provides a clear, actionable blueprint for reducing electronic waste at home. The website will serve as a go-to resource for individuals who want to minimize their environmental footprint by extending the life of electronics, recycling responsibly, and buying sustainable tech. Key features include:

  • 📱 What e-waste is and why it's a fast-growing environmental crisis.
  • 🛠️ Actionable tips: repair, upgrade, maintain devices to prolong lifespan.
  • ♻️ Local & online recycling/donation directories + data safety guides.
  • 🛒 Guidance on purchasing repairable, sustainable electronics (brand scorecards).
  • ✅ Interactive decision tree: "Repair, Donate, Recycle, or Keep?"
  • 🗺️ Curated list of certified e-waste recyclers and community take-back events.

The ultimate goal is to convert eco-anxiety into positive action — helping households reduce toxic waste, conserve resources, and save money.

🧑‍🌾 Scenarios (Target Audience Questions)

🔋 “I have 3 old phones and a dead laptop in my closet. Where can I recycle them safely without risking my personal data? And can the laptop be repaired instead?”

eWiseHome will answer with a searchable map of certified recyclers, step-by-step data wiping instructions, and a "repair or recycle?" decision flowchart tailored to device age and damage.

💡 “I want to buy a new tablet, but I care about sustainability. Which brands offer spare parts, repair guides, and long-term software updates?”

The website includes a buyer’s guide to repairable electronics (iFixit scores, right-to-repair champions), plus recommendations for certified refurbished products and eco-labels.

🏠 “What’s the simplest daily habit to reduce e-waste at home besides recycling?”

Practical micro-tips: extending charge cycles, using energy-saving modes, donating functional electronics to schools, and hosting community repair swaps — all featured in a digestible checklist.

🎨 Color Schema

Primary Green: #2B6E3C – headings, navigation background, buttons, key accents.
Warm Accent: #F4A261 – call-to-action, hover states, decision tree highlights, icons.
Background Soft: #EEF5E8 – page backdrop, subtle section separation.
Dark Forest: #1E2F2F – body text, footer, high-contrast readability.

Application: The green (#2B6E3C) creates a trustworthy, natural identity. Orange (#F4A261) draws users to interactive tools like the checklist and recycling locator. Light backgrounds (white cards on #EEF5E8) ensure accessibility and calm reading experience.

✒️ Typography

🔹 Headings & Site Title: Montserrat (Bold/SemiBold) – clean, modern, confident.

🔹 Body text, paragraphs, buttons, navigation: Open Sans – highly legible, neutral, optimized for screens.

🔹 Optional UI accents: monospace for resources (keeps technical clarity). Only 2 primary fonts: Montserrat (headings) + Open Sans (everything else). Max 3 fonts respected.

🌳 Decision Tree (repair/donate/recycle)

A flowchart-style interactive widget will guide users: "Is the device functional?" → "Does it have resale/repair value?" → options: repair, donate, recycle, keep. This matches planned content item #5 and will be prominently displayed on the homepage.

🗺️ Local & online resources

Planned integration: location-based search (Earth911 API concept) and vetted directories like Call2Recycle, E-Stewards, and manufacturer take-back programs. Links to repair cafes and donation centers will be included.
